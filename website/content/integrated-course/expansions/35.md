## Distinguish a model's fit from its truth everywhere
QUESTION: Under which conditions does this scientific model work well?
CORE: A model selects features of the world to explain or predict. Its success in one range does not grant unlimited scope. Ask what was measured, what assumptions simplified the situation, and where predictions have been tested. A model can be highly useful while incomplete. When moving between astronomy, psychology, engineering, and everyday life, check whether the relevant variables and scales still match.
CONNECT: [[11 Evidence, science, and causal claims]] evaluates evidence for a claim. [[20 Strategy, competition, and cooperation]] warns against transferring an analogy as a recipe.
EXAMPLE: A simple orbit diagram can teach the relation between bodies while leaving out perturbations needed for precise navigation.
DO: Choose a familiar model and list one useful prediction and two omitted factors.
TRY: Name a setting where the omissions would matter more.
REFLECT: Did I mistake a model's clarity for unlimited reach?
CHECK: What is the scope of a model?::The conditions and questions for which its assumptions and tests make it useful.
MODEL: Identify~Name the model and purpose.|Bound~List assumptions and range.|Apply~Use it only where fit is justified.

## Read a technical result through its uncertainty
QUESTION: How precise is the result, and what kind of uncertainty remains?
CORE: Measurements and estimates can vary because of instruments, sampling, model assumptions, or natural variation. Read a reported number with its unit, method, range, and context. A small numerical difference may be meaningless; a broad interval may still inform a decision. Distinguish uncertainty that more data could reduce from uncertainty caused by a poor model or missing variable. Do not turn an error range into a claim that nothing can be known.
CONNECT: [[09 Observation, bias, and calibrated judgment]] asks for calibrated confidence. [[26 Economics, incentives, and public choices]] shows why public numbers need interpretation.
EXAMPLE: Two estimates of attendance differ by a few people. If both use rough counts, the difference may not justify a claim that one program outperformed the other.
DO: Annotate a hypothetical statistic with unit, method, range, and missing context.
TRY: State which uncertainty would matter for the decision at hand.
REFLECT: Did I use an exact-looking number to avoid discussing how it was produced?
CHECK: What should accompany a technical number?::Its unit, method, relevant uncertainty, and the decision context in which it is used.
MODEL: Read~Identify unit and method.|Bound~Describe uncertainty.|Decide~Ask whether precision is enough for the purpose.

## Separate a mechanism from an observed association
QUESTION: Does the evidence show how a change produces an effect?
CORE: An association can suggest a question but may reflect a confounder, reverse direction, or selection. A proposed mechanism describes intermediate steps and should lead to additional checks. Even a plausible mechanism does not by itself prove the effect occurs at a meaningful size in real settings. Compare alternatives and, where ethically possible, look for evidence from interventions or natural contrasts. Keep causal language proportionate to the method.
CONNECT: [[11 Evidence, science, and causal claims]] introduces confounding. [[21 Practical problem solving and experiments]] uses a small test to distinguish explanations.
EXAMPLE: People using a study app score higher. They may have started more motivated; observing how the app changes practice and comparing similar learners would strengthen the causal case.
DO: Turn an association claim into two competing causal explanations.
TRY: Name one observation or safe test that would distinguish them.
REFLECT: Was the mechanism evidence, an explanation, or only a plausible story?
CHECK: Why is a plausible mechanism insufficient to establish causation?::The effect and its size still need evidence that addresses confounding and alternative explanations.
MODEL: Observe~State the association.|Explain~List mechanisms and rivals.|Test~Seek discriminating evidence.

## Approach a specialist field through prerequisite questions
QUESTION: What must I understand before a dense reference becomes useful?
CORE: Specialist books assume language, mathematics, methods, and professional context. Identify the question you need to answer, then work backward to the prerequisites and a suitable introductory source. Separate conceptual understanding from permission to perform a procedure. Use diagrams, worked examples, and a glossary to check comprehension, but confirm any consequential application with a qualified expert and current standards. Difficulty is a signal to sequence learning, not proof that the field is impossible.
CONNECT: [[07 Learning, memory, and understanding]] builds from prior knowledge. [[37 Health literacy and responsible care]] keeps specialist interpretation tied to qualified care.
EXAMPLE: Before reading a chemical reactor reference, a learner studies conservation, units, and simple rate concepts, then asks a qualified instructor about real equipment.
DO: Choose a difficult topic and list five prerequisites in a plausible order.
TRY: Write a question that distinguishes understanding a concept from carrying out a professional procedure.
REFLECT: Which missing prerequisite made the original text seem more mysterious than it was?
CHECK: How should a reader enter a specialist field?::Start with a precise question and its prerequisites, while respecting the boundary between literacy and professional practice.
MODEL: Ask~Define the learning goal.|Sequence~Build prerequisites.|Consult~Use expert guidance for consequential application.
