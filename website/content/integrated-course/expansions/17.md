## Separate information exchange from bargaining
QUESTION: What should I learn before trading concessions?
CORE: A negotiation has several jobs: understand interests, explore options, compare terms, and make an agreement. Rushing to a number before learning what matters can create a false conflict. Ask about timing, quality, risk, and constraints before assuming that price is the only issue. Share relevant information honestly while protecting legitimate privacy. Then make proposals that both parties can evaluate. A stage is a guide to the work, not a rigid script for controlling another person.
CONNECT: [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] supports accurate understanding. [[12 Decisions, strategy, and systems]] helps compare options against your alternatives. Ethical bargaining uses information to find workable terms, not to manufacture false scarcity.
EXAMPLE: A client asks for a lower fee. You learn their main concern is uncertain delivery. A smaller pilot with defined milestones may address the concern better than an immediate discount.
DO: For one upcoming negotiation, write five questions about the other side's interests and constraints before listing your concessions.
TRY: Practice describing your own non-negotiable limits and flexible terms separately.
REFLECT: Which assumption about the other person's priority changed after information exchange?
CHECK: Why separate information exchange from bargaining?::Understanding interests and constraints first can reveal options that an immediate concession fight would hide.
MODEL: Learn~Explore interests and constraints.|Design~Generate options.|Trade~Compare concrete terms and alternatives.

## Compare concessions by value, not symmetry
QUESTION: Must both sides give up the same amount for an agreement to be fair?
CORE: A concession can cost one party little and matter greatly to the other, or the reverse. List each proposed change, its real cost to you, and its likely value to them. Ask rather than assuming their value. Fairness also includes informed consent, clear terms, and the ability to walk away from an unacceptable deal. Equal-looking concessions are not always equal in consequence.
CONNECT: [[26 Economics, incentives, and public choices]] describes gains from exchange. [[32 Ethics, fairness, and difficult choices]] adds duties that cannot be reduced to a price. The useful comparison is whether terms improve both sides' options without exploiting vulnerability.
EXAMPLE: A freelancer can move a delivery date by two days at little cost, while the client values an earlier draft more than a lower price. Trading the draft date for a revised review schedule may help both.
DO: List three possible terms in one negotiation and estimate each term's cost to you.
TRY: Ask what each term is worth to the other party and identify one mutually useful trade.
REFLECT: Did I assume fairness meant identical sacrifice or a workable informed agreement?
CHECK: Why can unequal-looking concessions be mutually fair?::Parties may value the same term differently, so a clear exchange can improve both options without equal sacrifice.
MODEL: List~Identify tradable terms.|Value~Compare each side's likely cost and benefit.|Agree~Record informed, deliverable terms.

## Use a calibrated question to reveal constraints
QUESTION: How can a question uncover a problem without pretending the other person must solve it for me?
CORE: Questions beginning with how or what can invite the other person to explain a constraint or implementation problem. Ask with real curiosity, and be prepared to hear that no easy solution exists. A question is not a magic phrase that transfers responsibility or guarantees cooperation. Follow the answer with your own proposal and a check that it works for both parties.
CONNECT: [[13 Language, questions, and clear expression]] helps avoid leading wording. [[17.08 Close with terms that can be delivered]] keeps the conversation tied to implementation rather than verbal agreement alone.
EXAMPLE: A customer requests an impossible deadline. You ask which milestone matters most and what can move. Their answer may reveal that a preview, not final delivery, is needed by Friday.
DO: Rewrite one defensive refusal as a sincere question about the underlying constraint.
TRY: Prepare a feasible alternative you could offer after hearing the answer.
REFLECT: Did my question create understanding or merely disguise a no?
CHECK: What is the useful purpose of a calibrated question?::To reveal interests and constraints so both parties can examine workable options, not to force agreement.
MODEL: Ask~Explore the constraint openly.|Listen~Take the answer seriously.|Offer~Propose a feasible next step.
BOOKS: B311 B130

## Confirm who will do what after agreement
QUESTION: Why can a negotiation feel finished while the work is still unclear?
CORE: Agreement on a headline term does not settle implementation. Specify deliverables, timing, responsibilities, dependencies, and what happens if a condition changes. Read the terms back in plain language. A written record can prevent memory differences, though the formality needed depends on the stakes. If the agreement relies on someone absent, check their authority and capacity before promising on their behalf.
CONNECT: [[19 Leadership, teams, and institutions]] explores accountability across roles. [[24 Business, customers, and useful value]] shows that an offer's value depends on delivery. Closing is a transition into coordinated work.
EXAMPLE: Two groups agree to share a venue. They then clarify who books it, pays the deposit, handles accessibility needs, and contacts participants if the date changes.
DO: Take a recent agreement and list any unstated owner, date, or dependency.
TRY: Write a five-line confirmation that another party could use to carry out the agreement.
REFLECT: Which term seemed obvious to me but might not be obvious to them?
CHECK: What turns a verbal deal into an implementable agreement?::Clear deliverables, owners, timing, dependencies, and a shared record of the terms.
MODEL: Summarize~Restate the agreed outcome.|Assign~Name owners and dates.|Confirm~Check the record with all affected parties.
