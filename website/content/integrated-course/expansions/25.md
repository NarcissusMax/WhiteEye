## Reconcile the record with reality
QUESTION: How do I know the numbers in a plan match what actually happened?
CORE: A budget predicts; a record describes transactions; a statement from a bank or provider offers an independent check. Compare them regularly and investigate differences rather than silently adjusting the plan to look accurate. Categorization choices matter, especially for shared or business expenses, but an elaborate system is not required to begin. A trustworthy record supports choices and can reveal fees, duplicate charges, or delayed payments. Protect sensitive details and seek qualified help where formal accounting is required.

EXAMPLE: A household expects a monthly utility payment but sees two charges; reconciliation catches the error before next month's plan is made.
DO: Compare a small sample of recorded transactions with a statement and explain any difference.
TRY: Set a realistic date for repeating the check.
REFLECT: Which financial story changed when I looked at actual transactions?
CHECK: What does reconciliation do?::It compares a personal or business record with independent transactions and investigates differences.
MODEL: Record~Capture transactions.|Compare~Match the outside statement.|Resolve~Explain and correct differences.

## Keep uncertainty visible in a money forecast
QUESTION: What could make a financial plan miss its target?
CORE: Income, expenses, interest, and timing can change. A single forecast hides those possibilities. Create a base case and a plausible difficult case, then ask what choices remain workable in both. Use current terms for any real borrowing or tax decision and confirm them with appropriate sources or professionals. The purpose of scenarios is to prepare a response, not to predict the future precisely or justify fear.
CONNECT: [[12 Decisions, strategy, and systems]] uses scenarios for decisions under uncertainty. [[26 Economics, incentives, and public choices]] shows how broad conditions can affect a personal plan without determining it.
EXAMPLE: A freelancer plans around typical monthly receipts and also tests whether essential bills can be met during two low-income months.
DO: Build two simple scenarios for an upcoming expense using different timing or income assumptions.
TRY: Name one adjustable action for the difficult scenario before it arrives.
REFLECT: Which assumption was doing most of the work in my optimistic plan?
CHECK: Why use scenarios in a financial plan?::They show how choices hold up under different plausible conditions without claiming to know the future.
MODEL: Vary~Change consequential assumptions.|Compare~Check what remains feasible.|Prepare~Choose a proportionate response.

## Compare fees and incentives before choosing help
QUESTION: How is a financial product or adviser paid, and how might that affect the recommendation?
CORE: Fees can be visible, recurring, contingent, or embedded in a product. Ask for the full cost in a form you can compare over the period you expect to use the service. Clarify what an adviser is authorized to do, how they are compensated, and which alternatives were considered. A persuasive presentation or prestigious title does not replace these questions. For a real investment, tax, insurance, or debt decision, check current regulated disclosures and obtain qualified advice where needed.
CONNECT: [[18 Manipulation, propaganda, and independent judgment]] examines pressure and framing. [[10 Logic, arguments, and fair disagreement]] asks whether a recommendation follows from relevant reasons.
EXAMPLE: Two savings services advertise similar results, but one has a recurring charge that changes the net outcome over several years.
DO: Make a comparison sheet for a hypothetical product with price, recurring fees, restrictions, and incentives.
TRY: Write three questions you would ask a provider before accepting a recommendation.
REFLECT: Which cost or incentive was easiest to overlook in the first presentation?
CHECK: Why ask how financial help is paid?::Compensation and total fees can affect recommendations and the net result for the person using the service.
MODEL: Disclose~Find all costs and incentives.|Compare~Use the same time horizon.|Verify~Check current terms and qualifications.

## Build a money review around decisions
QUESTION: What should a periodic review change, rather than merely report?
CORE: A useful review compares goals with cash flow, obligations, buffers, and risks. It asks which assumptions held, which changed, and which action follows. Avoid treating one favorable month as a new permanent baseline or one difficult month as a verdict on character. Write the next decision in plain terms and schedule the next check. When another person shares the consequences, make the process transparent enough for them to participate.
CONNECT: [[40 Integration and the life-practice capstone]] turns records into a repeated learning cycle. [[15 Relationships, consent, and mutual choice]] keeps shared resources tied to honest conversation.
EXAMPLE: After three months of variable income, a pair revises their spending plan and payment timing while keeping their long-term aim intact.
DO: Review a sample month or hypothetical ledger against three stated priorities.
TRY: Write one small change and the observation that would make you revise it.
REFLECT: Was the review helping a decision or just producing a score?
CHECK: What should a money review produce?::A clear decision grounded in actual records, changed assumptions, and the purpose the money serves.
MODEL: Compare~Review goals and records.|Decide~Choose one adjustment.|Return~Set the next review point.
