## Compare interpretations within a tradition
QUESTION: Who is speaking for a tradition, and what do others within it say?
CORE: A tradition is rarely a single voice. Communities differ by history, authority, translation, practice, and the question being asked. When someone describes what a religion teaches, identify the text, interpreter, and community involved. Compare positions on a specific issue before making a broad statement. Respecting diversity does not mean denying real doctrines or disagreements; it means describing them accurately.
CONNECT: [[33 Philosophy, knowledge, and the good life]] practices fair representation. [[39 History, citizenship, and public institutions]] examines how institutions preserve and contest interpretations.
EXAMPLE: Two readers of the same sacred passage may agree on its importance while differing about how literally it applies to modern social duties.
DO: Choose a hypothetical claim about a tradition and list what source and community information would clarify it.
TRY: Write a comparison question that does not assume one member speaks for all.
REFLECT: Which easy generalization disappeared when I asked who was interpreting?
CHECK: Why compare views within a religious tradition?::Texts and practices can be interpreted differently by communities and authorities within the same tradition.
MODEL: Locate~Name text and interpreter.|Compare~Find internal differences.|Describe~State the scope of the claim.

## Distinguish spiritual counsel from a factual guarantee
QUESTION: Is this advice offering meaning, a practice, or a promised outcome in the world?
CORE: Prayer, meditation, ritual, and moral teaching can be important to people. A claim that a practice guarantees a medical, financial, or physical outcome is a different kind of claim and needs different evidence. Ask what is promised, to whom, and under what conditions. Avoid turning lack of the promised result into blame for someone's faith or character. For consequential health or financial decisions, keep qualified care and current facts in view.
CONNECT: [[11 Evidence, science, and causal claims]] evaluates empirical predictions. [[37 Health literacy and responsible care]] protects choices where inaccurate promises can cause harm.
EXAMPLE: A community offers prayer and practical support to an ill member without claiming prayer replaces diagnosis or guarantees recovery.
DO: Classify three hypothetical statements as spiritual meaning, practice guidance, or testable prediction.
TRY: Rewrite one overconfident guarantee as an honest statement of what the practice can and cannot promise.
REFLECT: Did a meaningful tradition become a factual claim without evidence?
CHECK: What changes when spiritual advice promises a physical outcome?::It becomes an empirical claim with consequences that require evidence and should not replace appropriate care.
MODEL: Classify~Identify the kind of claim.|Check~Apply the relevant standard.|Care~Protect practical support and choice.

## Read a practice in its lived setting
QUESTION: How do people actually use this belief or ritual in ordinary life?
CORE: A text alone cannot show every way a community lives. Ask how a practice is taught, adapted, and connected to family, service, grief, celebration, or discipline. Listen to participants while recognizing that one person's experience is not the whole tradition. Avoid collecting practices as isolated techniques for self-improvement without understanding their meaning and limits. Participation should respect the community's norms and people's consent.
CONNECT: [[16 Family, development, and relational patterns]] examines practices across generations. [[36 Literature, story, and human possibility]] attends to voice and context.
EXAMPLE: A daily reading is also a family conversation and a way of remembering relatives; its significance exceeds the words on a page.
DO: Form three respectful questions about the social setting of a practice.
TRY: Compare a textual description with a first-person account without treating either as exhaustive.
REFLECT: What did I miss by treating a living practice as a portable productivity method?
CHECK: Why study a religious practice in context?::Its meaning and use depend partly on the people, relationships, history, and norms around it.
MODEL: Read~Understand the text or teaching.|Listen~Attend to lived accounts.|Situate~Describe the practice in context.

## Discuss belief across disagreement respectfully
QUESTION: How can people with different convictions share a decision without requiring conversion?
CORE: Shared life often requires agreement about action while beliefs remain different. Clarify the practical decision, each person's non-negotiable constraints, and any common reasons for cooperation. Do not demand that someone translate every conviction into your vocabulary before it counts, but make public effects and rights discussable. Distinguish respect for a person's freedom of conscience from agreement with every claim they make. Record what remains disputed.
CONNECT: [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] helps maintain contact through disagreement. [[32 Ethics, fairness, and difficult choices]] gives a structure for shared obligations.
EXAMPLE: Neighbors with different beliefs coordinate a food program by agreeing on access, safety, and volunteer roles while leaving theology unsettled.
DO: Write a shared action statement for a hypothetical mixed-belief group.
TRY: Identify one unresolved conviction and one practical rule all can understand.
REFLECT: Was I seeking cooperation or hidden agreement on every premise?
CHECK: What can make cooperation possible across belief differences?::A clear shared action, respected constraints, discussable public effects, and room for unresolved conviction.
MODEL: Define~Name the common task.|Respect~State constraints and rights.|Agree~Choose a workable action.
