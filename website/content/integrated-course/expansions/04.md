## Decide when to disengage
QUESTION: When is leaving a situation the responsible action?
CORE: Agency includes choosing not to continue an exchange. A conversation may no longer be useful when safety deteriorates, consent is withdrawn, or repeated attempts produce only escalation. State your limit in terms of what you will do and, where suitable, when you can return. Disengaging is not a universal answer; some obligations still require a later response, documentation, or help from another person. The aim is to leave a harmful or unproductive moment without pretending the underlying issue has resolved itself.
CONNECT: [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] describes when to pause and return. [[29 Safety, awareness, and de-escalation]] gives priority to distance and help in a threatening situation. A boundary is an action under your control, not a command that guarantees another person's behavior.
EXAMPLE: A discussion turns into repeated shouting. You say that you will pause and discuss the specific issue tomorrow when both people can speak. If the situation is unsafe, you prioritize leaving and seeking help rather than arranging a neat closing line.
DO: Identify one recurring exchange that becomes less productive after a recognizable point. Write the cue for pausing.
TRY: Draft a short exit statement and a responsible follow-up plan, including who needs to know.
REFLECT: What obligation remains after I leave the immediate situation?
CHECK: What makes disengagement responsible rather than avoidant?::It protects a legitimate limit and includes an appropriate later action when the underlying obligation remains.
MODEL: Recognize~Notice the point of escalation or lost consent.|Leave~Take the action within your control.|Return~Address remaining duties through a safer route.

## Repair your part without owning the whole conflict
QUESTION: How can I apologize for my action without accepting responsibility for everything that happened?
CORE: A careful repair names what you did, its effect, and the change you will make. It need not endorse every accusation or require the other person to forgive you immediately. Likewise, another person's harmful choice does not erase your own contribution. Separate each person's behavior and the conditions around it. This makes accountability more precise and protects against both defensiveness and self-blame without limits.
CONNECT: [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] supplies the mechanics of apology. [[16 Family, development, and relational patterns]] shows why repeated patterns can make ownership confusing. A specific apology can coexist with a clear boundary.
EXAMPLE: You interrupted a family member during a disagreement, and they insulted you afterward. You can acknowledge the interruption and commit to waiting your turn while also stating that insults are not acceptable in the next conversation.
DO: Write a repair statement for one small recent mistake: action, effect, and change. Remove excuses and global self-condemnation.
TRY: Write a separate sentence naming what is outside your responsibility, without using it to cancel your repair.
REFLECT: Did I confuse taking my share with taking the entire conflict?
CHECK: What belongs in a precise repair?::Your specific action, its effect, and the change you will make, without claiming control over another person's choices.
MODEL: Own~Name your action.|Repair~Address its effect and future behavior.|Bound~Keep separate what belongs to others.
BOOKS: B260 B275

## Negotiate a commitment before capacity breaks
QUESTION: How can I change an obligation honestly when my capacity changes?
CORE: A promise made under one set of conditions may become impossible or costly under another. Waiting silently until you fail deprives other people of options. Name the original commitment, the changed constraint, and the alternatives you can still deliver. Ask which outcome matters most. This is different from making a vague apology or treating every obligation as optional. A revised agreement should be explicit enough that both parties can plan around it.
CONNECT: [[06 Attention, time, and a workable day]] shows the actual capacity. [[17 Influence, persuasion, and negotiation]] helps discuss alternatives fairly. Agency works best when it makes limits visible early.
EXAMPLE: You promised to help at two events but a work shift changes. You contact the organizer before the first event, explain the conflict, offer one event or a preparatory task, and ask what would be most useful.
DO: Choose one commitment under strain. Write the original agreement, current capacity, and the earliest time you could raise the issue.
TRY: Propose two feasible alternatives, including what each would require from the other person.
REFLECT: What did I risk by waiting, and what did early notice make possible?
CHECK: What should a renegotiated commitment include?::The original promise, the changed constraint, specific alternatives, and a clear new agreement.
MODEL: State~Describe the promise and changed condition.|Offer~Present realistic alternatives.|Agree~Record the revised commitment.
BOOKS: B026 B275
