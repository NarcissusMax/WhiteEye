## See stocks, flows, and delays in a changing system
QUESTION: Why can a sensible action take time to change the result I care about?
CORE: A stock is something accumulated, such as savings, inventory, or practiced skill. Flows add to or subtract from it. Delays mean an action today may affect the stock or its visible measure later. If you react to every short-term fluctuation without allowing for the delay, you can overcorrect. Draw the stock, the inflows and outflows, and the observation you use to judge progress. The diagram is a simplification; check it against real records.
CONNECT: [[05 Habits and behavior design]] applies this to repeated actions. [[25 Money, records, and financial judgment]] applies it to cash reserves. The same language links personal and organizational decisions without claiming their details are identical.
EXAMPLE: A shop reduces purchasing but inventory remains high for a month because existing orders still arrive. Increasing the reduction again without noting that delay could create a shortage later.
DO: Choose one changing quantity in your life or work. Draw its main inflows, outflows, and likely delays.
TRY: Identify one observation you might misread because it responds slowly to an action.
REFLECT: Did I expect a flow change to transform the stock immediately?
CHECK: What is the difference between a stock and a flow?::A stock is an accumulated quantity; flows are the rates or events that add to or remove from it.
MODEL: Map~Name the accumulated quantity.|Trace~Identify inflows, outflows, and delays.|Wait~Review at a time scale that fits the system.

## Find a proximate objective
QUESTION: What useful target can I reach when the final ambition is still distant?
CORE: A distant goal may be too broad to guide this week's work. A proximate objective is challenging but close enough that you can specify actions, constraints, and evidence of completion. It should solve a real part of the larger problem rather than create a ceremonial milestone. Choose it after diagnosing the current obstacle. Reaching it may change what the next objective should be, so leave room to revise.
CONNECT: [[20 Strategy, competition, and cooperation]] asks where effort has leverage. [[08 Practice, performance, and adaptation]] translates that into a specific training target. A proximate objective connects ambition to capability.
EXAMPLE: 'Build a successful business' gives little direction. 'Interview ten potential customers and test whether three will pay for a small pilot' is a nearer objective that can change the plan.
DO: State one distant goal and the obstacle closest to your current capability.
TRY: Define a four-week objective with a result you can verify and a reason it matters to the larger goal.
REFLECT: Will completing this objective teach me something or merely let me tick a box?
CHECK: What makes an objective proximate?::It is close enough to act on and verify while resolving a meaningful part of the larger challenge.
MODEL: Diagnose~Find the present obstacle.|Aim~Choose a reachable meaningful result.|Learn~Use the result to choose the next step.

## Check for a chain-link bottleneck
QUESTION: Does the whole result depend on several parts working together?
CORE: Some systems behave like a chain: improving one part produces little benefit while another part remains below the necessary standard. Map the components and the minimum each must provide. Then look for the constraint that prevents the whole path from working. This differs from improving the part that is easiest to measure or most visible. Be careful with human systems: people are not interchangeable links, and a bottleneck may be created by poor coordination or unrealistic demands.
CONNECT: [[19 Leadership, teams, and institutions]] examines responsibility for the system. [[24 Business, customers, and useful value]] applies the idea to delivery. Changing one metric without the connected process may waste effort.
EXAMPLE: A clinic improves appointment booking but lacks enough staff to follow up results. More bookings alone cannot deliver better care. The connected service must be examined as a whole.
DO: Map one work process from request to result. Mark three components that must all function.
TRY: Identify the present constraint and one way to test whether relieving it improves the whole result.
REFLECT: Was I optimizing a visible part while another part limited the outcome?
CHECK: What is a chain-link system?::A system whose overall performance depends on several complementary parts, so a weak part can limit the value of improvements elsewhere.
MODEL: Map~List required components.|Locate~Find the limiting connection.|Test~Improve the whole path, not only one score.

## Use a decision threshold before results arrive
QUESTION: What evidence would make me continue, revise, or stop a plan?
CORE: A decision threshold names the result at which an action changes. Set it before you see the outcome when possible, because disappointment or excitement can move the standard afterward. The threshold should include measurement quality, costs, and stakes. A number alone may be misleading; combine it with qualitative warning signs and a review date. If new information reveals the threshold was badly chosen, revise it openly and record why.
CONNECT: [[09.13 Keep hindsight from rewriting the decision]] preserves the earlier standard. [[40 Integration and the life-practice capstone]] uses this method to make an experiment reviewable. Precommitment improves learning when it remains open to justified revision.
EXAMPLE: You pilot a paid workshop. You decide in advance what enrollment, participant feedback, and delivery cost would justify another session. Afterward, you can discuss a surprising result without quietly changing the definition of success.
DO: For one upcoming low-stakes trial, write continue, revise, and stop conditions before it begins.
TRY: Add a review date and one reason that could justify changing the threshold after new evidence.
REFLECT: Which result would I have been tempted to explain away without a prior standard?
CHECK: Why set a decision threshold in advance?::It makes the response to evidence less vulnerable to wishful revision after the result is known.
MODEL: Define~Choose evidence and thresholds.|Observe~Run the trial and record results.|Decide~Apply or openly revise the standard.
