## Set a challenge limit before motivation takes over
QUESTION: What conditions would make continuing practice unwise today?
CORE: A difficult goal can invite meaningful effort and also make a person ignore signals that need attention. Before a demanding session, define the intended challenge, support available, and reasons to stop or modify it. A memoir of extraordinary endurance can motivate questions about persistence, but it is not a training prescription for a different body or situation. Review performance over time, including recovery and ordinary functioning.
CONNECT: [[27 Physical training, recovery, and nutrition literacy]] examines bodily warning signs. [[09 Observation, bias, and calibrated judgment]] warns against copying an exceptional story as a general rule.
EXAMPLE: A runner sets an effort goal and also agrees to stop for worsening unusual pain, rather than treating every stop as weakness.
DO: Write a challenge goal and two conditions for modifying a hypothetical practice session.
TRY: Identify who could give competent feedback if those conditions appear.
REFLECT: Was I using ambition to make a safety question feel shameful?
CHECK: Why set a challenge limit in advance?::It preserves judgment about safety and recovery when effort and identity make stopping harder to consider.
MODEL: Define~Name the intended challenge.|Bound~Set modification conditions.|Review~Compare effort with recovery.

## Select feedback near the decision it should improve
QUESTION: When and from whom will feedback be specific enough to use?
CORE: Feedback is more actionable when it follows a visible attempt and addresses a decision or movement that can be changed. Ask a qualified observer for what they noticed, what effect it had, and one next adjustment. Immediate correction can help a simple skill; reflection after the attempt may serve a complex performance better. Avoid accumulating so many comments that none can be practiced. Check whether the change improves performance in the real setting.
CONNECT: [[07 Learning, memory, and understanding]] uses feedback to locate an error. [[28 Sport, movement, and supervised skill]] tests transfer beyond a drill.
EXAMPLE: A speaker asks a colleague to note where listeners became confused in a practice talk, then revises one transition before the next attempt.
DO: For one skill, define the next attempt, observer, and one feedback question.
TRY: Choose a single change to test and a sign it helped.
REFLECT: Was I seeking a judgment of my ability or information about the next attempt?
CHECK: What makes performance feedback actionable?::It describes a specific attempt and a change that can be tested in the relevant setting.
MODEL: Attempt~Produce observable work.|Ask~Seek specific feedback.|Test~Change one element and review.
