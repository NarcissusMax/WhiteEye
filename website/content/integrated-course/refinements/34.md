## Hold an outside explanation beside an inside account
QUESTION: How can I compare a research explanation with what practitioners say a practice means?
CORE: An outside account may examine cognition, social effects, or history. An inside account may describe worship, obligation, transcendence, or community. Neither should be silently substituted for the other. State what each explains, how it was obtained, and what it does not address. Ask whether disagreement is about evidence, meaning, or the very question being asked. This permits critical study without treating participants as unaware of their own practice or treating testimony as proof of every external claim.
CONNECT: [[31 Mind, behavior, and consciousness]] separates reported experience from theory. [[33 Philosophy, knowledge, and the good life]] clarifies kinds of questions.
EXAMPLE: A participant describes a daily prayer as devotion; a researcher studies its effect on attention. Both accounts may be informative without being equivalent.
DO: Write two short accounts of a hypothetical practice, one first-person and one analytical, labeling their questions.
TRY: Identify one claim neither account can establish alone.
REFLECT: Which account had I treated as a replacement for the other?
CHECK: Why compare inside and outside accounts?::They can answer different questions about meaning, experience, function, and evidence.
MODEL: Hear~State the participant's account.|Study~State the analytical account.|Relate~Mark overlap and limits.
