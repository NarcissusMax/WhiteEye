## Give attention without performing charm
QUESTION: Am I interested in the person, or in being seen as interesting?
CORE: Connection grows when attention follows what the other person actually says and does. Ask a question, remember the answer, and share enough of yourself to make the exchange mutual. A social technique can help start a conversation, but constant performance can turn the other person into an audience. Notice whether both people have room to choose the pace and topic. Some relationships will not deepen, and that is not a failure to optimize.
CONNECT: [[13 Language, questions, and clear expression]] provides better questions. [[01 Self-knowledge and your starting point]] helps notice approval-seeking in the interaction.
EXAMPLE: At a gathering, a person asks a newcomer about a project they mentioned, listens, and shares a related experience without steering every turn back to a prepared story.
DO: Write two follow-up questions that depend on a real answer rather than a scripted sequence.
TRY: In one low-stakes conversation, notice whether you are listening or preparing your next impression.
REFLECT: Did the exchange give both people a place, or did I stage a performance?
CHECK: What distinguishes attention from charm performance?::Attention responds to the other person's actual words and choices instead of using them mainly to display oneself.
MODEL: Ask~Invite a real answer.|Follow~Respond to what was said.|Share~Keep the exchange mutual.

## Build friendship through ordinary reliability
QUESTION: What repeated actions show that this relationship is safe and mutual?
CORE: Friendship is not only a powerful conversation or shared interest. It also rests on turning up, respecting a no, remembering a commitment, and being able to repair a mistake. Notice patterns over time without keeping a moral ledger of favors. A person may care but have different availability; discuss expectations instead of testing them. Reliability should include your own limits so the relationship remains sustainable.
CONNECT: [[04 Agency, responsibility, and boundaries]] makes promises realistic. [[16 Family, development, and relational patterns]] shows how repair can strengthen trust.
EXAMPLE: Two friends cannot meet often, but they state when they are available and follow through on plans they make.
DO: List three ordinary actions that make one friendship feel dependable.
TRY: Name one expectation worth discussing rather than silently measuring.
REFLECT: Was I looking for dramatic proof of care while ignoring repeated ordinary behavior?
CHECK: What builds ordinary friendship reliability?::Repeated follow-through, respect for limits, honest expectations, and repair after mistakes.
MODEL: Notice~Observe patterns over time.|Agree~Discuss expectations.|Keep~Follow through and repair.
