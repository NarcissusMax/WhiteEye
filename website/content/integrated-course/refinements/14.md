## Separate the factual correction from the hurt
QUESTION: Can I correct a detail while still attending to the person's experience?
CORE: A conflict may contain an inaccurate statement and a valid concern. If you correct the detail first and stop there, the other person may hear a refusal to address the effect. If you avoid correction entirely, the record may become misleading. Name the specific fact, acknowledge the experience you understand, and ask what repair or change matters. Do not claim to know another person's feelings better than they do.
CONNECT: [[09 Observation, bias, and calibrated judgment]] separates fact from interpretation. [[13 Language, questions, and clear expression]] uses precise wording to keep two issues visible.
EXAMPLE: You did send the message on Tuesday, but it arrived after the colleague's deadline. You clarify the time and still address the missed handoff.
DO: Rewrite a defensive factual correction to include the practical impact.
TRY: Ask one question about the needed repair without conceding an untrue detail.
REFLECT: Did my correction solve the shared problem or only protect my image?
CHECK: Why separate correction and hurt in conflict?::A factual error can be corrected while the person's experienced impact still deserves attention.
MODEL: Clarify~State the checkable fact.|Acknowledge~Name the effect.|Repair~Agree on a useful next step.

## Prepare a difficult conversation around a request
QUESTION: What do I need the other person to understand and decide?
CORE: A useful opening gives the topic, one concrete observation, its effect, and a request for discussion. Avoid a long prosecution of past incidents or a surprise verdict on character. Choose a setting and time that allow both people to respond, unless urgency requires faster action. Listen for information that changes your first account, and close with a clear decision or next conversation. Preparation creates a starting point, not a script that controls the other person.
CONNECT: [[17 Influence, persuasion, and negotiation]] clarifies interests. [[22 Planning, collaboration, and delivery]] turns any agreement into owners and timing.
EXAMPLE: A housemate asks to discuss shared bills, names two late payments and their effect, then asks how to make the next month workable for both.
DO: Draft a four-sentence opening with topic, observation, impact, and request.
TRY: List two questions that leave room for facts you do not know.
REFLECT: Was I preparing to understand and decide, or to deliver a finished accusation?
CHECK: What belongs in a difficult conversation opening?::A clear topic, concrete observation, effect, and workable request with room for the other person's account.
MODEL: Prepare~Name the issue and request.|Discuss~Hear new information.|Close~Agree on next steps.
