## Distinguish restoration from numbing
QUESTION: Does this activity help me return to life, or postpone what matters until the cost grows?
CORE: Rest may be quiet, playful, social, or absorbing. Numbing can look similar from the outside but repeatedly leaves an important need or task untouched and may reduce your ability to respond. Avoid judging an activity by appearance alone. Ask what you were seeking, how you felt afterward, and whether the pattern supports recovery. If a behavior feels hard to control or causes significant harm, seek appropriate support rather than relying on a course exercise.
CONNECT: [[06 Attention, time, and a workable day]] protects genuine recovery. [[05 Habits and behavior design]] looks at the function a repeated behavior serves.
EXAMPLE: Watching a program after work helps one person unwind; for another, an automatic all-night session becomes a way to avoid a difficult call and leaves them exhausted.
DO: Compare two recent breaks by need, duration, and effect on the next action.
TRY: Design one small alternative for a recurring avoidance pattern without removing needed rest.
REFLECT: Which activity did I call recovery without checking its longer pattern?
CHECK: What helps distinguish restoration from numbing?::Its function and repeated effect on recovery, important needs, and the ability to return to chosen action.
MODEL: Ask~Name the need.|Observe~Check the after-effect.|Adjust~Protect rest while addressing avoidance.

## Use a quiet pause to hear the real concern
QUESTION: What becomes clearer when I stop adding input for a moment?
CORE: A pause does not solve a problem by itself. It can, however, create room to notice an emotion, a competing demand, or an assumption that was hidden by constant messages and activity. Keep the pause short and chosen; some people find silence difficult, and there is no single correct ritual. Write a question afterward and decide whether the next step is action, more information, rest, or help.
CONNECT: [[06 Attention, time, and a workable day]] limits inputs deliberately. [[13 Language, questions, and clear expression]] converts a diffuse feeling into a question.
EXAMPLE: Before replying to a tense message, someone takes five quiet minutes and realizes they need to clarify a deadline rather than defend their character.
DO: Take a brief pause before a low-stakes response and note the first concern that appears.
TRY: Write one question that would help you act on the concern rather than circle around it.
REFLECT: Did stillness reveal useful information, or was I expecting it to provide certainty?
CHECK: What is a useful purpose of a quiet pause?::To notice a concern or assumption clearly enough to choose an appropriate next step.
MODEL: Pause~Reduce input briefly.|Name~Write the concern.|Choose~Act, investigate, rest, or seek support.
