## Take field notes that separate seeing from guessing
QUESTION: What could I record now that memory might later rearrange?
CORE: A field note preserves concrete details, timing, and uncertainty before a later story forms. Record what was seen or heard, then mark interpretations as interpretations. Keep the note short enough to make during real life and protect other people's privacy. When reviewing, look for omitted context and what changed your understanding. This is especially useful when a decision depends on a sequence of events, not on one dramatic impression.
CONNECT: [[09 Observation, bias, and calibrated judgment]] begins with description. [[21 Practical problem solving and experiments]] uses records to locate when a result changes.
EXAMPLE: A team notes when requests arrive, when they are assigned, and when they are completed before guessing which person causes delays.
DO: Write a brief hypothetical field note with time, observed detail, and a separately labeled inference.
TRY: Add one missing context question you would check later.
REFLECT: What detail would I likely have filled in from expectation if I had waited a week?
CHECK: What makes a field note useful?::It preserves dated observations and uncertainty separately from later interpretation.
MODEL: Record~Capture the concrete event.|Separate~Mark inference clearly.|Revisit~Check missing context.

## Test a remembered lesson against the page
QUESTION: Did I learn the author's claim, or a simpler version I made up?
CORE: Retrieval practice is valuable, but a remembered answer can be confidently wrong or strip away qualifications. Explain the idea from memory first, then compare with the passage or worked example and mark differences. Note what the text actually supports and where your own application begins. This prevents both passive rereading and false certainty from an easy flashcard. If an original source is contested, accurate recall still does not make its claim true.
CONNECT: [[11 Evidence, science, and causal claims]] separates a source's claim from its support. [[33 Philosophy, knowledge, and the good life]] asks for fair representation before criticism.
EXAMPLE: A learner recalls a chapter as saying intuition always fails; the text's narrower point concerns specific conditions for intuitive judgment.
DO: Recall a source idea in three sentences without looking, then compare it with the original passage.
TRY: Write one qualification or counterexample your first recall omitted.
REFLECT: Which simplification made the lesson easier to remember but less accurate?
CHECK: Why compare retrieval with the source?::Recall can expose learning and also reveal missing qualifications or mistaken claims.
MODEL: Retrieve~Explain from memory.|Compare~Check the source.|Correct~Keep scope and support visible.
