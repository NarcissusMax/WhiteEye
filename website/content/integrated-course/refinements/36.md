## Give one clue more than one plausible reading
QUESTION: What else could this action mean if my first story is wrong?
CORE: A narrative clue is an observed detail plus an interpretation. In a collaborative mystery, later writers can make an earlier detail support a different coherent account. Readers can use that structure to practice generating alternatives without denying the detail. Keep alternatives constrained by what the story actually shows and update them when new information arrives. The exercise also reminds us that real people's motives are not directly visible from one action.
CONNECT: [[09 Observation, bias, and calibrated judgment]] separates observation from inference. [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] asks rather than assuming another person's motive.
EXAMPLE: A character avoids a meeting. Fear, a secret task, or an ordinary scheduling conflict could fit until later scenes narrow the possibilities.
DO: Choose a clue in a story and write two interpretations that fit the facts so far.
TRY: Identify one later detail that would distinguish the interpretations.
REFLECT: Which story felt inevitable only because I had stopped considering alternatives?
CHECK: Why give a clue multiple readings?::It keeps the observed detail separate from a favored interpretation until evidence narrows the possibilities.
MODEL: Observe~State the clue.|Branch~Generate plausible readings.|Update~Use later evidence to narrow them.
