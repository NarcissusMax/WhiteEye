## Plan for a surprise without pretending to predict it
QUESTION: What would I do if an important assumption changed suddenly?
CORE: A scenario plan is useful when it identifies a trigger and a response, not when it dramatizes every imaginable catastrophe. Choose two plausible changes that would alter the decision, such as a lost resource or a new opportunity. Define an early signal, options you could preserve now, and whom you would need to inform. Revisit the plan as facts change. Preparedness should reduce fragility without consuming the resources needed for ordinary life.
CONNECT: [[29 Safety, awareness, and de-escalation]] uses proportionate preparation. [[22 Planning, collaboration, and delivery]] turns a contingency into roles and communication.
EXAMPLE: A community project considers what it will do if its venue becomes unavailable and identifies a backup location and a message plan.
DO: For one project, choose two plausible surprises and one decision each would change.
TRY: Write a trigger and first response for the more consequential scenario.
REFLECT: Did the scenario protect options or just produce an alarming story?
CHECK: What makes a scenario plan actionable?::A plausible changed condition, an observable trigger, preserved options, and a feasible first response.
MODEL: Vary~Change one major assumption.|Prepare~Keep an option open.|Trigger~Act when the signal appears.

## Keep a reserve for the constraint that matters
QUESTION: Which capacity must remain available when the plan meets reality?
CORE: A reserve may be time, money, equipment, attention, goodwill, or a second route. It has a cost, so protect it where a shortage would stop the whole effort or create harm. A reserve is not evidence of a timid plan; it can make a responsible commitment possible. State what the reserve is for and when it may be used, rather than letting it be consumed by routine expansion.
CONNECT: [[25 Money, records, and financial judgment]] plans for uneven cash needs. [[06 Attention, time, and a workable day]] leaves space for unannounced work.
EXAMPLE: An event team leaves one volunteer unassigned during setup so urgent access issues can be handled without pulling every station apart.
DO: Identify the most fragile resource in one plan and the cost of its failure.
TRY: Design a modest reserve or alternate route with a clear use condition.
REFLECT: Did I call the plan efficient because it had no room for variation?
CHECK: When is a reserve strategically useful?::When a shortage in a critical resource would stop the whole plan or impose a large cost.
MODEL: Locate~Find the fragile constraint.|Protect~Set aside capacity.|Use~Define the trigger for deploying it.
