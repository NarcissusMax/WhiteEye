## Build a reset point into a habit
QUESTION: What happens after a week that does not fit the usual routine?
CORE: A habit plan is fragile if its only rule is never miss. Choose a simple reset point tied to an ordinary event: the next morning, the next weekly review, or the first available work block. Decide which minimum action restarts the practice and which conditions call for a changed plan. Do not repay every missed repetition with an exhausting session. Recovery is part of a useful habit, especially when life circumstances change.
CONNECT: [[06 Attention, time, and a workable day]] keeps plans inside actual capacity. [[08 Practice, performance, and adaptation]] distinguishes repeated action from safe progress.
EXAMPLE: A learner misses three study days during travel. Their reset rule is to answer five recall questions on the next available morning, then resume the normal schedule.
DO: Define a reset trigger and minimum action for one routine.
TRY: Write a rule for when repeated misses should prompt redesign rather than more resolve.
REFLECT: What story about failure made a simple return feel too small?
CHECK: Why include a reset point in a habit?::It gives a feasible return after disruption and a way to redesign a pattern that no longer fits.
MODEL: Trigger~Choose a return moment.|Restart~Do the minimum useful action.|Review~Redesign if disruption repeats.

## Audit whether a habit still serves its purpose
QUESTION: Is this routine helping the value that created it?
CORE: Repetition can continue after its original purpose changes. At a review point, identify the value, the behavior, its actual effect, and any cost that has grown. Keep, adjust, or stop the routine based on current evidence. A streak may be satisfying, but it is not proof that the routine remains helpful. Changing a habit thoughtfully is different from abandoning a commitment at the first difficult moment.
CONNECT: [[02 Values, meaning, and a direction worth choosing]] supplies the purpose. [[12 Decisions, strategy, and systems]] asks whether a measure has replaced the real outcome.
EXAMPLE: A daily posting routine built a small audience, then began crowding out the creative work the audience wanted. The creator reduces posting to protect the underlying goal.
DO: Write the purpose and current effect of one established habit.
TRY: Decide one condition for keeping, adjusting, or ending it at the next review.
REFLECT: Was I protecting the purpose or the evidence that I could keep a streak?
CHECK: When should a habit be reviewed?::When its purpose, effects, or costs may have changed enough to alter the right action.
MODEL: Recall~Name the original purpose.|Compare~Check current effects and costs.|Decide~Keep, adjust, or stop.
