## Ask what a belief does without claiming why it is true
QUESTION: How can I study a belief's psychological role while respecting the question of its truth?
CORE: A belief can organize identity, community, memory, or action. Studying those roles does not settle whether the belief's central claim is true or false. Likewise, a person's sincerity does not prove the claim. Separate descriptive questions about how belief forms and functions from philosophical, historical, or empirical questions about what it asserts. Avoid reducing believers to one psychological cause or treating all beliefs as interchangeable.
CONNECT: [[34 Religion, spiritual traditions, and belief]] examines traditions on their own terms. [[11 Evidence, science, and causal claims]] matches evidence to the kind of claim.
EXAMPLE: Researchers may ask how a shared ritual supports belonging; participants may also ask whether the ritual expresses a real spiritual truth. The questions are related but distinct.
