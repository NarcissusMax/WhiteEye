## Set a return point beyond the first success
QUESTION: How will I know whether this change remains useful after the course ends?
CORE: A successful trial can fade when ordinary demands return, and a disappointing trial may improve after revision. Choose a future date, a small set of observations, and a person or record that will help you review honestly. State what would count as continued value and what would prompt change. Do not turn the review into permanent self-surveillance. The course's final product is a practice you can revisit, including the freedom to stop a method that no longer serves its purpose.
CONNECT: [[05 Habits and behavior design]] builds reset points. [[12 Decisions, strategy, and systems]] keeps a plan reviewable when assumptions change.
EXAMPLE: Three months after a study experiment, a learner checks whether the new routine still supports understanding and leaves room for rest, then adjusts it for a changed timetable.
DO: Add a future review date and three observations to your capstone plan.
TRY: Write one condition for continuing, adjusting, and stopping the method.
REFLECT: What would make me keep a method because it once worked, even after its purpose changed?
