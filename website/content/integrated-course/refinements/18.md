## Examine pressure on the way a choice is framed
QUESTION: Which options or meanings have been made hard to imagine?
CORE: Manipulation may work by shaping the question before the person compares answers. Notice who defined the options, what is omitted, and whether refusal is portrayed as disloyal or foolish. Ask for a wider set of choices and a pause to consult independent information. Do not assume every persuasive frame is malicious; evaluate the pattern, power, and consequences. A person under sustained pressure may need trusted support, not another demand to 'think clearly' alone.
CONNECT: [[10 Logic, arguments, and fair disagreement]] reveals hidden premises. [[32 Ethics, fairness, and difficult choices]] examines power and consent.
EXAMPLE: A manager says staff must choose between total unpaid overtime and not caring about clients. The team identifies other staffing and service options.
DO: Rewrite a forced-choice message with at least two omitted options.
TRY: Name a safe independent source or person to consult before deciding.
REFLECT: Which part of the frame made another option feel morally unavailable?
CHECK: How can a frame limit choice?::It can hide feasible options or attach shame to refusal before the person evaluates the actual alternatives.
MODEL: Reveal~Name the stated options.|Widen~Find omitted choices.|Check~Seek independent support.

## Recover your own judgment after a pressured decision
QUESTION: What can I learn without blaming myself for someone else's pressure?
CORE: After a pressured choice, distinguish the information and options you had then from what you know now. Record the tactics or conditions that narrowed choice, the actual effects, and the support needed next. Avoid a hindsight story that makes the pressure seem obvious or makes the target responsible for another person's conduct. A useful review strengthens future boundaries and support systems; it does not require the person to relive details for a course exercise.
CONNECT: [[04 Agency, responsibility, and boundaries]] separates responsibility from blame. [[29 Safety, awareness, and de-escalation]] supports proportionate help when there is ongoing risk.
EXAMPLE: Someone agreed to a purchase under repeated urgency. They review the terms and options for remedy with trusted help, then create a pause rule for similar requests.
DO: Analyze a hypothetical pressured choice with facts known then, pressure used, and support available now.
TRY: Write one future pause or consultation rule without assigning blame to the target.
REFLECT: Am I using hindsight to learn or to punish the earlier self?
CHECK: What makes a post-pressure review fair?::It distinguishes earlier information from later knowledge, names the pressure, and builds support without shifting blame for coercion.
MODEL: Reconstruct~State what was known.|Support~Find appropriate help.|Prepare~Set a future boundary.
