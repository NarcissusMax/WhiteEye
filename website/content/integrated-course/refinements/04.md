## Ask for help while retaining a decision
QUESTION: What can another person contribute without becoming responsible for my whole choice?
CORE: Asking for help can be an exercise of agency. Define the question, what you have tried, and the kind of help that would be useful: information, feedback, time, practical support, or a decision shared by agreement. Be clear about who will choose and carry out the next step. A helper may also have limits. Avoid pretending independence means never needing people, or treating a suggestion as a command you must obey.
CONNECT: [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] supports a useful conversation. [[17 Influence, persuasion, and negotiation]] clarifies roles and workable commitments.
EXAMPLE: A student asks a mentor to compare two study plans and explain the trade-offs, then chooses one and owns the schedule.
DO: Draft a help request naming the decision, prior attempt, and specific assistance wanted.
TRY: State what part of the decision remains yours and what would be shared.
REFLECT: Was I avoiding help to look strong, or seeking it to avoid choosing?
CHECK: How can asking for help preserve agency?::By defining the needed contribution while keeping decision rights and next actions clear.
MODEL: Specify~Name the help needed.|Agree~Clarify roles and limits.|Choose~Use the input in your own decision.

## Honor support with a clear next step
QUESTION: What should I do after someone has given useful help?
CORE: Receiving support can create gratitude and a practical responsibility to follow through, but not a debt that gives the helper control over your life. Summarize what you understood, say which action you will take, and report back when appropriate. If advice does not fit, explain your decision honestly rather than pretending agreement. This makes help more useful and lets both people learn whether it worked.
CONNECT: [[07 Learning, memory, and understanding]] uses teach-back to check understanding. [[15 Relationships, consent, and mutual choice]] keeps reciprocity free from scorekeeping.
EXAMPLE: A colleague spends time showing a process. You demonstrate the first step, ask one remaining question, and later tell them what happened.
DO: Write a short follow-up to a hypothetical helper: what you learned, what you will do, and when you will review.
TRY: Write a respectful version for a case where you decide against the suggestion.
REFLECT: Did I leave the helper believing I agreed when I had already chosen otherwise?
CHECK: What is a clear response to useful help?::State what you understood, what you will do, and any relevant follow-up without surrendering your own choice.
MODEL: Receive~Clarify the advice.|Decide~Choose honestly.|Follow~Report the relevant result.
