## Let gratitude and dissatisfaction coexist
QUESTION: Can I appreciate what is present while still asking for change?
CORE: Gratitude notices support, opportunity, or a good moment; it does not prove every condition is acceptable. Dissatisfaction can identify a real mismatch with a value. Hold both observations without forcing one to cancel the other. This prevents gratitude from becoming a demand to tolerate harm and prevents ambition from erasing what already sustains you. Turn the mismatch into a bounded choice where you have room to act.
CONNECT: [[03 Emotions, acceptance, and a steadier response]] allows mixed feelings. [[12 Decisions, strategy, and systems]] turns a concern into a decision rather than an endless verdict on life.
EXAMPLE: A worker values supportive colleagues and also needs a sustainable workload. Appreciation for the team can accompany a request to change assignments.
DO: Write one thing you value in a situation and one change you still need.
TRY: Identify a first conversation or action that respects both facts.
REFLECT: Which feeling had I used to invalidate the other?
CHECK: How can gratitude and change work together?::Appreciation can describe what is good while dissatisfaction identifies a real need for action.
MODEL: Notice~Name what supports you.|Name~State the mismatch.|Act~Choose a bounded step.

## Define success in the life it should serve
QUESTION: What would an achievement enable, and what might it displace?
CORE: A visible milestone can be useful without being the final purpose. Ask what capability, security, contribution, or freedom the milestone is meant to create. Then compare the path to it with the life you want during the pursuit. Some costs are worth choosing; others quietly destroy the reason for the goal. Revisit the definition when circumstances change rather than measuring your life against a standard adopted years ago.
CONNECT: [[25 Money, records, and financial judgment]] asks what money is for. [[23 Creative work and sustained projects]] asks which scope helps a project reach its real audience.
EXAMPLE: A promotion would increase income, but its hours would end a valued care commitment. The person compares a slower path and a different role, not only the salary figures.
DO: For one goal, finish the sentence: 'I want this because it would let me...'
TRY: List two costs of the route and one alternative route worth examining.
REFLECT: Am I defending a milestone or the life it was meant to support?
CHECK: What should a success measure be tied to?::The capability or life purpose it serves, along with the costs of the path to it.
MODEL: Purpose~Name the underlying good.|Cost~Examine what pursuit displaces.|Choose~Compare routes and revise.
BOOKS: B160 B221 B294
