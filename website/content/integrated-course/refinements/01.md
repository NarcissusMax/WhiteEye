## Notice when belonging becomes a performance
QUESTION: Where am I changing myself to earn a place, and what does it cost?
CORE: Most people adapt to a group; adaptation is not automatically dishonest. The question is whether you are choosing what to share or repeatedly hiding a need, value, or limit because you believe acceptance depends on it. Look for a pattern across settings rather than judging one awkward moment. You can test a small, safe act of candor with someone who has earned trust. Belonging should be examined through actual relationships, not a rule that every thought must be disclosed.
CONNECT: [[15 Relationships, consent, and mutual choice]] treats connection as mutual. [[04 Agency, responsibility, and boundaries]] helps protect a chosen limit without withdrawing from people.
EXAMPLE: A student keeps agreeing to extra plans to appear easygoing, then has no time for the work they value. They test a clear, ordinary refusal with a trusted friend.
DO: Name one setting where you perform ease or certainty and one need or preference you conceal.
TRY: Write a low-risk honest sentence you could use there, without demanding a particular response.
REFLECT: Which relationships make it easier to be accurate about myself?
CHECK: What distinguishes chosen adaptation from costly performance?::Whether it helps participation while preserving important needs and values, or repeatedly hides them to secure approval.
MODEL: Notice~Identify a repeated performance.|Choose~Select a safe act of candor.|Observe~Learn from the actual response.

## Map identity claims to current evidence
QUESTION: Which descriptions of me are still useful, and which belong to an older chapter?
CORE: People carry labels from school, family, work, and past failures. A label can summarize experience but may outlive the behavior it describes. Write the claim, when it was formed, present evidence for and against it, and the action it now encourages. Do not replace an unwanted label with an unsupported flattering one. A more accurate working description can leave room for both strengths and development.
CONNECT: [[09 Observation, bias, and calibrated judgment]] asks for evidence rather than a vivid example. [[05 Habits and behavior design]] lets identity follow repeated action.
EXAMPLE: Someone called themselves unreliable after missing deadlines in a chaotic job; their current record in a different setting shows both completed work and a continuing need for planning.
DO: Choose one long-held self-label and list two current observations supporting or limiting it.
TRY: Rewrite it as a specific, revisable description tied to a behavior and context.
REFLECT: What action did the old label make seem impossible or unnecessary?
CHECK: Why revisit a self-label?::A label may persist after its evidence or context changes and can narrow future choices.
MODEL: Trace~Find the label's origin.|Check~Compare current evidence.|Revise~Use a specific working description.
BOOKS: B108 B294 B221
