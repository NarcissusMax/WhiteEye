## Add institutions to an economic model
QUESTION: Which rules and organizations determine how a general incentive works here?
CORE: An economic model may explain a tendency while leaving out banking rules, labor arrangements, local administration, or public trust. State the model's mechanism, then identify institutions that could strengthen, weaken, or redirect it. Compare real cases cautiously; a country's history is not an interchangeable laboratory. When a policy seems to have failed, distinguish whether the mechanism was wrong, the institution blocked it, or the goal was measured too narrowly.
CONNECT: [[39 History, citizenship, and public institutions]] provides context for policy. [[35 Science, models, and technical literacy]] asks where a model's assumptions fit.
EXAMPLE: Lower borrowing costs may have different effects where firms lack viable projects, households carry heavy debt, or banks face different constraints.
DO: Choose one simple policy claim and list three institutional conditions it assumes.
TRY: Describe how changing one condition might alter the predicted effect.
REFLECT: Was I treating a general tendency as a guaranteed result in a particular place?
