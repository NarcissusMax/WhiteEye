## Use humor without making a person the cost
QUESTION: Does this joke invite connection, or require someone else to absorb embarrassment?
CORE: Humor depends on context, power, history, and whether the other person has room to opt out. A line can feel playful to its speaker and cutting to its target. Prefer shared absurdity, self-directed lightness, or observation that leaves dignity intact. Notice the response and be willing to stop or repair without arguing that your intention proves no harm occurred. The aim is a more relaxed conversation, not a demonstration of cleverness.
CONNECT: [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] uses the other person's response as information. [[32 Ethics, fairness, and difficult choices]] treats dignity as a constraint on social skill.
EXAMPLE: At a meeting, a light comment about the confusing printer eases tension; a joke about a new colleague's accent would place the cost on a person.
DO: Rewrite a hypothetical teasing remark so the humor does not depend on a person's vulnerability.
TRY: Name a cue that would make you stop or apologize even if you meant the line kindly.
REFLECT: Was I trying to connect or to win the moment?
CHECK: What makes conversational humor considerate?::It fits context, respects power and dignity, and responds to how the other person actually receives it.
MODEL: Choose~Aim at shared context.|Notice~Read the response.|Repair~Stop or acknowledge harm when needed.

## Use the other person's stated criteria
QUESTION: What does the person say matters to them about this choice?
CORE: Persuasive language is clearer when it addresses criteria the listener has actually named, such as reliability, cost, time, or learning. Ask instead of guessing a personality type from a few words. Repeat their criterion in plain language and explain how a proposal does or does not meet it. If your offer cannot meet the criterion, say so. This makes communication relevant without turning an unverified profiling framework into a diagnosis or covert tactic.
CONNECT: [[17 Influence, persuasion, and negotiation]] prepares interests and alternatives. [[14 Listening, conflict, and repair]] checks that you understood the concern.
EXAMPLE: A colleague says they need predictable handoffs. You discuss who owns each step and how changes will be signaled, not a generic speech about innovation.
DO: Write three open questions that uncover a person's decision criteria.
TRY: Draft an honest proposal response to one stated criterion, including a limitation.
REFLECT: Did I listen for a real condition or look for a phrase to exploit?
CHECK: How should someone's criteria guide language?::Ask for the criteria, check understanding, and address them honestly without pretending to know a hidden type.
MODEL: Ask~Learn stated priorities.|Check~Reflect them back.|Explain~Show fit and limits.
