## Distinguish behavior, experience, and explanation
QUESTION: What is observable, what is reported, and what is a theory about it?
CORE: Psychology studies behavior and reported experience using many methods. A theory proposes mechanisms, while a person's own account describes what an experience is like. Do not collapse these levels. The same behavior can arise through different paths, and a person may know the feeling better than the cause. A useful explanation should make a checkable difference or clarify a specific case.
CONNECT: Older psychoanalytic theories, cognitive models, and neuroscience texts ask overlapping questions but use different evidence and concepts.
EXAMPLE: Someone avoids a meeting. You can observe the absence, hear their account of anxiety, and consider several causal explanations without treating one as established.
DO: Sort three claims about one behavior into observation, report, and theory.
TRY: Ask what evidence would distinguish two proposed explanations.
REFLECT: Which level have I treated as if it were another?
CHECK: Why separate levels of psychological explanation?::Behavior, subjective report, and causal theory provide different kinds of information.
MODEL: Observe~Record behavior.|Listen~Hear experience.|Explain~Test a proposed mechanism.

## Understand emotion as information in decision-making
QUESTION: What might feeling contribute to a judgment?
CORE: Emotions can signal salience, bodily state, learned experience, and social meaning. They can also bias attention and intensify an unsupported story. Treat emotion as relevant information that needs interpretation rather than as either infallible truth or noise to suppress. A decision may require both feeling and explicit comparison of options.
CONNECT: Neuroscience accounts challenging a strict reason-emotion split meet the course's practical distinction between experience and inference.
EXAMPLE: Discomfort about a contract prompts you to inspect unclear terms. The feeling is useful as a cue to examine, not proof that the other party is deceptive.
DO: Describe a recent feeling and the question it prompted.
TRY: Check the external evidence before acting on the feeling's first explanation.
REFLECT: Have I dismissed a useful signal or granted it more certainty than it earned?
CHECK: How can emotion aid judgment?::It can direct attention to something important while still requiring interpretation and evidence.
MODEL: Feel~Notice salience.|Examine~Ask what it may signal.|Decide~Integrate evidence and values.

## Interpret the brain through careful models
QUESTION: What does this brain explanation actually establish?
CORE: Brain regions and neural processes contribute to perception, memory, emotion, and action, but a simple diagram rarely explains a whole personality or decision. Ask whether a claim comes from measurement, a model, or a metaphor. Avoid saying a brain scan proves a specific motive in one person. Neuroscience can constrain theories without replacing psychological and social context.
CONNECT: Popular brain-based self-help often makes broader claims than the underlying research. The philosophy of science module helps inspect that jump.
EXAMPLE: A book describes an “emotional brain” overriding a “rational brain.” The image may help discussion, but real decisions involve interacting systems and context.
DO: Identify one brain metaphor and the specific finding it is meant to summarize.
TRY: Write what the metaphor omits or may mislead a reader to assume.
REFLECT: Am I using technical vocabulary as proof without understanding the evidence?
CHECK: What is a limit of simple brain diagrams?::They simplify interacting processes and do not by themselves identify an individual's motive or destiny.
MODEL: Finding~Identify measured evidence.|Model~Understand the simplification.|Limit~Avoid overclaiming.

## Examine memory as reconstruction
QUESTION: How reliable is my recollection of a meaningful event?
CORE: Memory supports learning and identity but is affected by attention, later information, emotion, and retelling. A vivid memory can be sincerely held and still contain errors. Use contemporaneous records where accuracy matters, while respecting that a person's experience is not erased by uncertainty about details. Avoid using memory science as a blanket way to dismiss someone else's account.
CONNECT: Mnemonic techniques improve retrieval of chosen material, while autobiographical memory raises different questions about reconstruction and interpretation.
EXAMPLE: Two colleagues remember a meeting deadline differently. Checking the written agreement helps settle the practical issue without declaring either person dishonest.
DO: Compare one recollection with a dated record if available.
TRY: Note what you know, what you infer, and what remains uncertain.
REFLECT: Which part of my certainty comes from vividness rather than corroboration?
CHECK: Why can vivid memory still be uncertain?::Memory is reconstructed through attention and later interpretation rather than being a perfect recording.
MODEL: Recall~State the memory.|Corroborate~Check records and other evidence.|Calibrate~Keep uncertainty visible.

## Use psychological categories without self-diagnosis
QUESTION: Is this framework helping me understand a pattern or assigning an unsupported label?
CORE: Concepts such as defense mechanisms, attachment, cognitive bias, and personality can prompt useful questions. Their definitions, evidence, and clinical use differ. Do not diagnose yourself or another person from a single chapter, quiz, or conflict. Focus on observed behavior and its effects, and use qualified care for persistent distress or serious concerns.
CONNECT: The collection includes scholarly psychology, popular typologies, and dramatic “dark psychology” books. Their claims require different scrutiny.
EXAMPLE: You notice a habit of explaining away feedback. You investigate when it happens instead of deciding that you have a permanent disorder.
DO: Turn one psychological label into a specific, observable pattern.
TRY: Identify what the label does and does not explain.
REFLECT: Does the category expand useful choices or narrow my view of the person?
CHECK: What is a responsible use of a psychological category?::As a limited question or description supported by evidence, not a casual diagnosis.
MODEL: Concept~Know its scope.|Behavior~Observe actual patterns.|Care~Seek expertise when needed.

## Compare competing accounts of consciousness
QUESTION: What is each theory trying to explain about experience?
CORE: Consciousness discussions may address wakefulness, awareness of the world, self-awareness, felt experience, or the relation of mind and body. Specify the target before comparing theories. Neuroscience can examine correlates and mechanisms; philosophy asks what those findings imply and what remains unexplained. A speculative theory should be presented as such even when it uses scientific vocabulary.
CONNECT: Damasio, Freud, older speculative physics, and spiritual accounts cannot be collapsed into one proof. They disagree in method and claim.
EXAMPLE: A study of brain activity during perception informs how awareness works, but it does not settle every philosophical question about subjective experience.
DO: Define two different meanings of consciousness and a question for each.
TRY: Compare one empirical and one philosophical claim by evidence and scope.
REFLECT: Which problem did I assume was solved because the same word appeared in both texts?
CHECK: Why define the target of a consciousness theory?::Different accounts may explain different phenomena and require different kinds of evidence.
MODEL: Target~Define the phenomenon.|Method~Identify the inquiry.|Limit~State what remains open.

## Recognize stagecraft behind apparent mind reading
QUESTION: Could an impressive demonstration arise from information, suggestion, or selection?
CORE: Mentalism and cold reading can be entertaining performances. They do not establish telepathy or access to private thoughts. Examine what the performer knew, how questions shaped responses, which misses were omitted, and whether a prediction was specific in advance. Enjoy the performance while preserving the distinction between stage effect and empirical ability.
CONNECT: The collection includes mentalism instructions and paranormal claims. Studying their mechanisms supports critical literacy without turning deception into a relationship technique.
EXAMPLE: A broad statement feels personal because the listener supplies the details. A controlled test would need predictions recorded before feedback and count failures too.
DO: Analyze a harmless public demonstration for prior information, feedback, and selection.
TRY: Design a fair test of its claimed ability without performing deceptive readings on someone.
REFLECT: Which detail made the effect feel impossible while the missing data stayed invisible?
CHECK: Does a convincing mind-reading performance prove telepathy?::No. Stagecraft, information, suggestion, and selective memory can produce convincing effects.
MODEL: Claim~State the supposed ability.|Alternatives~Look for ordinary mechanisms.|Test~Count all predictions fairly.

## Keep a plural but disciplined mind model
QUESTION: Which explanation helps this problem without pretending to explain everything?
CORE: Biological, psychological, social, and philosophical accounts can illuminate different levels of a person's life. Use the model matched to the question: a sleep problem, a communication conflict, and a metaphysical question require different methods. Models may interact, but they should not be blended into an unfalsifiable story. Preserve uncertainty and seek qualified help for serious symptoms.
CONNECT: This module widens the frame for ethics and philosophy, where facts about minds inform but do not alone determine what should be done.
EXAMPLE: A person's concentration difficulty may involve workload, sleep, task design, and health. One dramatic label is unlikely to capture all relevant factors.
DO: Map one difficulty at biological, personal, relational, and institutional levels.
TRY: Identify which level you can investigate next and which needs specialist input.
REFLECT: Which favored explanation have I stretched beyond its evidence?
CHECK: What makes a plural mind model disciplined?::It matches each explanation to a specific question, evidence, and limit rather than claiming one lens explains all.
MODEL: Levels~Identify possible factors.|Evidence~Check each claim.|Action~Choose a proportionate next inquiry.
